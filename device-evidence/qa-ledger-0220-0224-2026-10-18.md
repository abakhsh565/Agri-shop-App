## Why the two are recorded rather than applied

**`0220` — superseded.** QA applied the narrower staging variant on 2026-10-17; the repository now
carries the statements production recorded. Compared statement-by-statement: the seeding is
byte-identical; the difference is two post-condition assertions. QA's variant asserts about the rows
*this file created*, production's about *every* membership — and QA legitimately holds multi-shop
memberships, so the wider assertion can never pass there. Recorded pair: `9817e2aa5a92…` (ledger) /
`270c81c1315c…` (file).

**`0224` — not applicable.** Its backfills reference `purchases.outstanding_payable` and
`purchase_items.cost_price`, columns that exist on production and never on QA. Measured on QA: the
effect it exists to produce is already present — `sale_submissions` exists, and `create_sale`,
`create_purchase`, `adjust_stock` all take `payload jsonb` and call `guard_operational_request(...)`.

## Follow-up: 0232, and the stale storage assertion

The first CI run after the batch was green on `migrate verify` and red in three integration files,
all reporting `permission denied for view marketplace_listings` (42501). `0223` had revoked `anon`
from every relation in `public`, including the curated view whose anon `SELECT` is the ledger's own
ACL (stated by `0185`, re-issued by `0210`, re-applied at the end of `0214`) and whose public
readability the boundary suite describes as intentional.

`0232_the_curated_view_is_the_public_door_again.sql` restores that one grant, asserts anon can read
the view, holds nothing on any other relation in `public`, and holds no write on the view. Applied
through the tool and recorded; `migrate verify --env staging` PASS.

`tests/integration/securityAudit.test.ts` had asserted that at least four `public_all_*` storage
policies exist — the vulnerability `0230` closed. It now asserts the invariant instead: no
anon/public write policy, no client `UPDATE`/`DELETE` policy, and exactly `images public read/SELECT`
plus `images auth write/INSERT`.
